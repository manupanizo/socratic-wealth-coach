# wealth principles — coach's reference

This file is a fallback, not a starting point.

Use it only when a user is genuinely stuck articulating their own view on a topic and needs an example to react to. The primary approach is to ask open questions and let the user's answers build the principles file in their own words. See Phase 2 in the skill for the elicitation questions to lead with.

When you do draw on this file, offer individual examples as prompts — "some people think about it this way, does that resonate or does your instinct go somewhere different?" — not as a list to walk through. The examples below are not correct answers. They are representative stances that real people hold, offered to give the user something to push against.

---

## Example principles — the nature of wealth

- Wealth means having passive income that exceeds expenses; the gap between them is the measure of freedom
- Wealth is about optionality — having choices you wouldn't otherwise have
- Wealth that increases stress or reduces security isn't actually wealth
- Wealth isn't static; it requires active management or it erodes over time
- Wealth doesn't define a person's worth

## Example principles — the wealth target

- The wealth journey has an end condition: net worth sufficient — in size and liquidity — to cover expenses for whatever scope the person defines (their own life, a partner's, dependents until a certain age)
- Generational wealth beyond that is an explicit choice, not a default
- A specific number or life condition is more useful than a vague sense of "enough"

## Example principles — relationship with money

- Understanding money management and financial systems is foundational to building wealth
- Past experiences and upbringing shape financial behavior in ways worth making explicit
- Avoiding high-interest debt and managing liabilities matters for long-term outcomes

---

## Open questions to draw on if stuck

- *What does "being wealthy" actually mean to you?*
- *What would financial security look like for your specific life?*
- *What's the condition under which you'd feel you have "enough"? Who is in scope — just you, a partner, dependents?*
- *What's a financial tradeoff you'd refuse to make for more money?*
- *What does money give you that you value most — freedom, security, options, something to pass on?*
- *Is there anything about how you were raised around money that you want to consciously carry forward — or leave behind?*
